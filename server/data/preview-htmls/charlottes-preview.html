MUST DO

Character Inside-Out

a) Choose Wilbur, Fern, or Charlotte and draw the outline of their character. • Inside the outline: write feelings and thoughts • Outside the outline: write actions and behaviour from Chapters 1-15

This is a hands-on activity. Try it on paper!

Consider the main events involving your chosen character in Chapters 1-15. What were they thinking and feeling at different moments? How did their actions and behavior show these internal states? Use specific examples from the book.
✅ Solution
A completed character outline for Wilbur, Fern, or Charlotte. The inside of the outline should contain relevant feelings and thoughts attributed to the chosen character in Chapters 1-15. The outside should list specific actions and behaviours performed by the character during those chapters, demonstrating understanding of their personality and role.
MUST DO

Comic Strip

b) Create a 6-8 panel comic strip showing the scene: • Wilbur meeting Charlotte • The barn animals talking • Charlotte's plan to save Wilbur Choose one of your own! Add speech bubbles and captions.

This is a hands-on activity. Try it on paper!

Recall the key moments from Chapters 1-15 where Wilbur first meets Charlotte, the other barn animals discuss him, and Charlotte reveals her cunning plan. Use expressive drawings, dialogues in speech bubbles, and descriptive captions to tell the story clearly.
✅ Solution
A comic strip with 6-8 panels. It should clearly depict Wilbur meeting Charlotte, barn animals engaging in dialogue, Charlotte formulating her plan to save Wilbur, and one additional scene chosen by the student. Each panel should include appropriate speech bubbles for character dialogue and captions for contextual information or narration.
MUST DO

Vocabulary Collector

c) Select 2 interesting words from each of the chapters. For each word, find and create: • Meaning (in your own words) • Sentence from the story OR your own sentence

This is a hands-on activity. Try it on paper!

As you reread Chapters 1-15, pay attention to unfamiliar, vivid, or important words. Choose words that you find interesting or that help you better understand the story. Remember to explain the meaning in your own words and then demonstrate its use in a sentence.
✅ Solution
A collection of words, with two interesting words selected from each of Chapters 1-15. For each chosen word, its meaning should be clearly explained in the student's own words. Additionally, a sentence (either directly quoted from the story or an original sentence created by the student) demonstrating the correct usage of the word should be provided.
MUST DO

Book-to-Life Connection

d) Make a personal connection: • A time you helped a friend • A time you felt scared yet supported Explain how it connects to the story using story evidences.

Answer =
Think about the strong friendships and moments of support in Charlotte's Web, particularly between Wilbur and Charlotte. Consider instances where characters showed courage or found comfort. How do your own experiences of helping a friend or feeling supported during fear mirror these themes and events? Use specific examples from Chapters 1-15 to illustrate your connections.
✅ Solution
A written response that describes a personal experience of helping a friend and another personal experience of feeling scared yet supported. For both experiences, a clear explanation is provided on how the experience connects to specific themes, characters, or events in Charlotte's Web (Chapters 1-15), supported by relevant 'story evidences' from the text.
MUST DO

Point of View Switch

e) Rewrite a short scene from a different character's perspective: • Fern watching Wilbur at the barn • Templeton observing Charlotte's plan Write 1-2 paragraphs.

Answer =
Choose either Fern or Templeton. Imagine what they would personally see, hear, feel, and think during the chosen scene. How would their unique personality, knowledge, and role in the story influence their narration and interpretation of the events? Focus on their internal monologue and sensory details.
✅ Solution
A rewritten short scene (1-2 paragraphs) from Chapters 1-15, either Fern watching Wilbur at the barn or Templeton observing Charlotte's plan. The scene should be narrated entirely from the chosen character's perspective, effectively conveying their thoughts, feelings, and unique observations during that moment.
MUST DO

Barnyard Newspaper

f) Create a newspaper page with: • Headline about Wilbur • One short news report • One opinion column by an animal • An illustration or ad (Templeton-approved)

This is a hands-on activity. Try it on paper!

Imagine you are an animal in the barnyard reporting on the exciting events related to Wilbur from Chapters 1-15. What would be the most important news headline? What kind of short report would inform the barnyard residents? What opinions might another animal express? What illustration or ad would the shrewd Templeton find appealing and approve of?
✅ Solution
A created newspaper page featuring a prominent headline about Wilbur (e.g., 'Amazing Pig Stuns Barnyard!'). It should include one short news report covering a key event from Chapters 1-15 (e.g., Charlotte's first web message or Wilbur's arrival). An opinion column written from the perspective of another barn animal (e.g., a sheep, goose, or cow), expressing their views. Finally, an illustration or advertisement that aligns with Templeton's character or interests, such as one promoting delicious leftover scraps or a cozy, hidden storage spot.
