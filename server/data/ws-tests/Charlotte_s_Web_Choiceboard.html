📝 Note

After reading Chapters 1-15 of Charlotte's Web, select and complete 4 out of the 6 tasks from the choice board that best show your understanding of the text.

MUST DO

Character Inside-Out

📐 Stylized clipboard icon

1) Choose Wilbur, Fern, or Charlotte and draw the outline of their character. Inside the outline, write their feelings and thoughts. Outside the outline, write their actions and behaviour from Chapters 1-15.

This is a hands-on activity. Try it on paper!

Consider the key events and challenges each character faces in Chapters 1-15. How do they react emotionally and physically to these events? What are their key thoughts and visible actions?
✅ Solution
A complete submission would include a drawn outline of either Wilbur, Fern, or Charlotte. Inside the outline, there would be specific feelings (e.g., Wilbur: fear, joy, loneliness; Fern: curiosity, empathy, concern; Charlotte: wisdom, responsibility, protective love) and thoughts (e.g., Wilbur: 'Will I be saved?', 'I love Fern'; Fern: 'Wilbur is special', 'I want to protect him'; Charlotte: 'I must save Wilbur'). Outside the outline, there would be actions and behaviors (e.g., Wilbur: eating slops, trying to spin a web, being scared; Fern: visiting the barn daily, observing animals quietly; Charlotte: spinning webs, talking to Wilbur, planning to save him) clearly linked to Chapters 1-15.
MUST DO

Comic Strip

📐 Stylized clipboard icon

1) Create a 6-8 panel comic strip showing one of the following scenes from Chapters 1-15: Wilbur meeting Charlotte, The barn animals talking, Charlotte's plan to save Wilbur, or choose one of your own significant scenes. Add speech bubbles and captions.

This is a hands-on activity. Try it on paper!

Focus on key dialogue and actions within your chosen scene. Use speech bubbles for what characters say and captions for narration or setting details. Remember to use 6-8 panels for a clear sequence.
✅ Solution
A complete submission would be a 6-8 panel comic strip illustrating a chosen scene from Chapters 1-15 (e.g., Wilbur's arrival and Charlotte's introduction, animals discussing Wilbur's fate, or Charlotte's first web-writing idea). The comic strip should include appropriate speech bubbles for character dialogue and captions to describe setting or actions, effectively conveying the narrative of the chosen scene.
MUST DO

Vocabulary Collector

📐 Stylized clipboard icon

1) Select 2 interesting words from each of Chapters 1-15 of Charlotte's Web. For each selected word, provide its meaning in your own words and use it in a sentence from the story OR your own sentence.

Chapter 1: 'runt' - Meaning: The smallest or weakest of a litter. Sentence: Wilbur was a runt, and Fern saved him from being killed.
As you read Chapters 1-15, pay attention to unfamiliar or vivid words. Choose words that help you understand the story better. For the meaning, try to explain it simply. For the sentence, you can quote directly or create one that shows you understand the word's use.
✅ Solution
A complete submission would include a list of words (at least 2 per chapter from 1-15, totaling 30 words), each with a clear meaning explained in the student's own words, and a sentence demonstrating proper usage. The sentence can either be a direct quote from the book or an original sentence that accurately reflects the word's meaning within the context of the story or a new context.
MUST DO

Book-to-Life Connection

📐 Stylized clipboard icon

1) Make a personal connection to the story by describing: (1) A time you helped a friend, and (2) A time you felt scared yet supported. For both connections, explain how it relates to events or characters in Charlotte's Web, using specific story evidences from Chapters 1-15.

Think about instances in the story where characters show friendship (e.g., Charlotte helping Wilbur) or experience fear and receive support (e.g., Wilbur being scared but comforted by Charlotte/Fern). Connect these to your own experiences, using details from the book to show the parallel.
✅ Solution
A complete submission would describe a personal experience of helping a friend, clearly linking it to an event or character interaction in Charlotte's Web (e.g., Fern saving Wilbur, Charlotte saving Wilbur). It would also describe a personal experience of feeling scared but supported, connecting it to a specific moment in the book (e.g., Wilbur's fear of being butchered, Fern's comfort). Both connections must be supported by 'story evidences' - specific examples or details from Chapters 1-15.
MUST DO

Point of View Switch

📐 Stylized clipboard icon

1) Rewrite a short scene from Chapters 1-15 of Charlotte's Web from a different character's perspective. Choose either: Fern watching Wilbur at the barn OR Templeton observing Charlotte's plan. Write 1-2 paragraphs.

When rewriting, consider what your chosen character (Fern or Templeton) would notice, feel, and think during the scene. How would their unique personality, motivations, and knowledge shape their description of events? Focus on their internal monologue and sensory details from their perspective.
✅ Solution
A complete submission would be 1-2 paragraphs rewriting a chosen scene (Fern watching Wilbur OR Templeton observing Charlotte's plan) from the chosen character's perspective. The writing should clearly reflect the character's voice, thoughts, feelings, and observations, showing an understanding of their personality and role in the story as presented in Chapters 1-15. For example, Templeton's perspective might be more cynical or self-interested, while Fern's might be more innocent and observant.
MUST DO

Barnyard Newspaper

📐 Stylized clipboard icon

1) Create a newspaper page titled 'The Barnyard Bugle' (or similar) related to Chapters 1-15 of Charlotte's Web. Your newspaper page should include: a headline about Wilbur, one short news report, one opinion column written by an animal character, and an illustration or advertisement (Templeton-approved).

This is a hands-on activity. Try it on paper!

Imagine you are an editor for a newspaper read by barn animals. What's the most important news about Wilbur? What would an animal like Templeton, the Goose, or the Sheep write about? How can you make your illustration or ad fit the barnyard theme?
✅ Solution
A complete submission would be a creative newspaper page (can be drawn or digitally created) including a catchy headline related to Wilbur from Chapters 1-15 (e.g., 'Remarkable Pig Discovered!'), a short news report detailing an event, an opinion column written from the perspective of an animal character (e.g., Templeton's cynical view on humans, the Goose's ramblings), and an illustration or advertisement relevant to the barnyard theme or story events (e.g., an ad for slops, a picture of Wilbur).
