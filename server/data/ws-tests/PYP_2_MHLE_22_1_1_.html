Addition and Subtraction

Combining numbers (addition) or taking one number away from another (subtraction).

📚

Regrouping

The process of exchanging values between place values (e.g., 1 ten for 10 ones, or 1 hundred for 10 tens) during addition or subtraction.

📚

Word Problems

Mathematical problems presented in a story or real-world context, requiring students to determine the correct operation to solve them.

📝 Note

The Home Learning Engagement has been divided into three levels: Must Do: These are essential engagements which you need to complete. Could Do: These engagements are optional but recommended. Enrichment Task: These are bonus engagements to explore your interests further.

Task 2 · Solve the following word problems using the appropriate number operation.

a) There are 507 crows in the field. A loud sound scares 224 of them away. How many crows are left?

Answer = crows
When something is 'scared away' or 'leaves', it indicates a subtraction operation.
✅ Solution
To find out how many crows are left, subtract the number of crows that flew away from the initial number of crows: 507 - 224 1. Ones place: 7 - 4 = 3 2. Tens place: 0 - 2. Regroup from hundreds: 5 becomes 4, 0 becomes 10. So, 10 - 2 = 8. 3. Hundreds place: 4 - 2 = 2. Total crows left: 283.

b) A market sells 476 apples in one week and 348 apples in the next week. How many apples have been sold in total?

Answer = apples
To find a 'total', you need to combine the amounts from both weeks using addition.
✅ Solution
To find the total number of apples sold, add the number of apples sold in the first week to the number sold in the next week: 476 + 348 1. Ones place: 6 + 8 = 14. Write down 4, carry over 1. 2. Tens place: 7 + 4 + 1 (carried over) = 12. Write down 2, carry over 1. 3. Hundreds place: 4 + 3 + 1 (carried over) = 8. Total apples sold: 824.

Task 3 · Work out these 2 step addition and subtraction word problems.

a) Leena has a collection of 673 books. She gives 126 books to Meena and 258 books to Teena. How many books are left with her now?

Answer = books
This is a two-step problem. First, find the total number of books given away. Then, subtract that total from the initial number of books Leena had.
✅ Solution

Step 1: Find the total number of books Leena gave away. Books given to Meena + Books given to Teena = 126 + 258 1. Ones: 6 + 8 = 14 (write 4, carry 1) 2. Tens: 2 + 5 + 1 (carry) = 8 (write 8) 3. Hundreds: 1 + 2 = 3 (write 3) Total books given away = 384.
Step 2: Find the number of books left with Leena. Initial books - Books given away = 673 - 384 1. Ones: 3 - 4. Regroup from tens (7 becomes 6, 3 becomes 13). 13 - 4 = 9. 2. Tens: 6 - 8. Regroup from hundreds (6 becomes 5, 6 becomes 16). 16 - 8 = 8. 3. Hundreds: 5 - 3 = 2. Books left with Leena = 289.

Task 4 · Identify the mistake. Write the correct working and explain the mistake.

a) Identify the mistake in the following subtraction problem, write the correct working, and explain the mistake: 203 - 57 ------- 373

Focus on each column (ones, tens, hundreds) and check if regrouping (borrowing) was done correctly when the top digit is smaller than the bottom digit.
✅ Solution
The correct working is: 2 0 3 - 5 7 ------- 1 4 6 Explanation of the mistake: 1. Ones place: To subtract 7 from 3, regrouping is needed. We borrow from the tens. Since the tens place is 0, we must regroup from the hundreds place. The 2 in the hundreds place becomes 1, and the 0 in the tens place becomes 10. Then, we borrow from the 10 in the tens place, making it 9, and the 3 in the ones place becomes 13. So, 13 - 7 = 6. The student got 3, indicating an error in this step. 2. Tens place: After regrouping, the tens place is 9. 9 - 5 = 4. The student got 7, which is incorrect. 3. Hundreds place: After regrouping, the hundreds place is 1. 1 - 0 = 1. The student got 3, which is incorrect. The mistake is a complete failure to apply the rules of regrouping (borrowing) correctly across multiple place values during subtraction, leading to an entirely incorrect result.

✅ Solution
To get the greatest answer from subtraction (A - B), number A must be the largest possible and number B must be the smallest possible. Available digits: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. 1. Form the largest 3-digit number (A): Use the largest three digits for the hundreds, tens, and ones places in descending order. The largest digits are 5, 4, 3. So, A = 543. 2. Form the smallest 3-digit number (B) from the remaining digits: The remaining digits are 1, 0, 2. To make the smallest 3-digit number, put the smallest non-zero digit in the hundreds place, followed by the smallest remaining digit, then the next smallest. So, B = 102. Now, subtract: 543 - 102 1. Ones: 3 - 2 = 1 2. Tens: 4 - 0 = 4 3. Hundreds: 5 - 1 = 4 The greatest answer when subtracting them is 441.

b) Which two digits can you subtract to get the greatest hundreds?

Answer =
Consider the hundreds digits of the largest and smallest 3-digit numbers you formed in the previous part. What is their difference?

Task 7 · Calculate the missing digits in these calculations.

a) Calculate the missing digits (from left to right, top to bottom) in this calculation: 9 6 _ - 8 _ 3 -------- 1 6 2
Start with the ones column. What number minus 3 equals 2? Then move to the tens column. What number minus the missing digit equals 6?
✅ 1. Ones column: `_ - 3 = 2`. The missing digit must be 5 (since 5 - 3 = 2). 2. Tens column: `6 - _ = 6`. The missing digit must be 0 (since 6 - 0 = 6). 3. Hundreds column: `9 - 8 = 1`. This confirms the calculation. The missing digits are 5 (top, units place) and 0 (bottom, tens place).
b) Calculate the missing digits (from left to right, top to bottom) in this calculation: 7 _ 5 - _ 3 _ -------- 6 7 0
Work from right to left (ones to hundreds). Remember to account for any borrowing needed or carried over.
✅ 1. Ones column: `5 - _ = 0`. The missing digit must be 5 (since 5 - 5 = 0). 2. Tens column: `_ - 3 = 7`. This implies regrouping. The top digit must have been 0 and became 10 after borrowing from the hundreds place. So, 10 - 3 = 7. The missing digit (top, tens place) is 0. 3. Hundreds column: The 7 in the hundreds place became 6 after lending to the tens place. So, `6 - _ = 6`. The missing digit (bottom, hundreds place) must be 0 (since 6 - 0 = 6). The missing digits are 0 (top, tens place), 0 (bottom, hundreds place), and 5 (bottom, units place).
c) Calculate the missing digits (from left to right, top to bottom) in this calculation: _ 8 9 - 2 _ _ -------- 2 6 4
Start with the ones column. What number subtracted from 9 equals 4? Continue working from right to left.
✅ 1. Ones column: `9 - _ = 4`. The missing digit must be 5 (since 9 - 5 = 4). 2. Tens column: `8 - _ = 6`. The missing digit must be 2 (since 8 - 2 = 6). 3. Hundreds column: `_ - 2 = 2`. The missing digit must be 4 (since 4 - 2 = 2). The missing digits are 4 (top, hundreds place), 2 (bottom, tens place), and 5 (bottom, units place).
d) Calculate the missing digits (from left to right, top to bottom) in this calculation: 6 _ 9 - _ 6 _ -------- 2 4 0
Work from right to left (ones to hundreds). Pay close attention to borrowing in the tens and hundreds columns.
✅ 1. Ones column: `9 - _ = 0`. The missing digit must be 9 (since 9 - 9 = 0). 2. Tens column: `_ - 6 = 4`. This implies regrouping. The top digit must have been 0 and became 10 after borrowing from the hundreds place. So, 10 - 6 = 4. The missing digit (top, tens place) is 0. 3. Hundreds column: The 6 in the hundreds place became 5 after lending to the tens place. So, `5 - _ = 2`. The missing digit (bottom, hundreds place) must be 3 (since 5 - 3 = 2).
