a) Imagine you sell bracelets. The supplies cost $15. If you sell them for $1.50 each, how many bracelets would you have to sell to make a profit? Show your thinking in two ways.

To make a profit, your total sales earnings must be greater than your initial cost of supplies. First, calculate the number of bracelets needed to just cover the $15 cost.
✅ Solution
To make a profit, the total amount of money you earn from selling bracelets must be more than the $15 you spent on supplies. Way 1: Calculate the break-even point and then add for profit. Cost of supplies = $15 Selling price per bracelet = $1.50 Number of bracelets needed to cover the cost (break-even point) = Total Cost / Price per bracelet = $15 / $1.50 = 10 bracelets. If you sell 10 bracelets, you will earn $15, which means you cover your costs but make no profit. To make a *profit*, you need to sell at least one more bracelet than the break-even point. So, 10 + 1 = 11 bracelets. Selling 11 bracelets: 11 * $1.50 = $16.50. Profit = $16.50 - $15 = $1.50. Way 2: Incrementally check sales until profit is achieved. Let's check the total earnings for different numbers of bracelets: - If you sell 9 bracelets: 9 * $1.50 = $13.50. This is less than $15, so you would still have a loss. - If you sell 10 bracelets: 10 * $1.50 = $15.00. This exactly covers your cost, so you make no profit (you break even). - If you sell 11 bracelets: 11 * $1.50 = $16.50. This is more than $15, so you make a profit ($16.50 - $15 = $1.50). Therefore, you need to sell 11 bracelets to make a profit.
